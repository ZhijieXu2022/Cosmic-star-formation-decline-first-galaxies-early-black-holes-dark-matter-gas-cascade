# Cosmic star-formation decline, the first galaxies and early black holes from the energy cascade of dark matter and gas

Xu Z., 2026, *Cosmic star-formation decline, the first galaxies and early black holes from the energy cascade of dark matter and gas*, Research Square preprint, [doi:10.21203/rs.3.rs-11556357/v1](https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-11556357/v1).

## Major findings

- Haloes of fixed mass collapse at a fixed density contrast until a crossover at a\* ≈ 0.5–0.64, after which they grow denser; the characteristic mass m_h\* ∝ a^{3θ}, with θ falling from 1 to 1/2 at a\*.
- Gas radiates its random energy; if its contrast stays fixed, the cascade rate shared by all galaxy cores rises as (1+z)^{5/2}.
- The rate at the effective radius lies below the core rate by a factor F ∝ M^{-2/3}: about 1 in spheroids and about 10² in low-mass discs.
- Late decline: after a\*, the fraction of infalling gas that cools efficiently falls as (1+z)^{-5/4}, steepening the local slope of the star-formation density from 1.5 to 2.7 with no added parameter.
- First galaxies: a sharp onset of feedback-free star formation at z ≈ 10.5 fits the JWST UV densities and luminosity functions better than bursty or feedback-free alternatives (ΔAIC ≥ 42).
- Black holes: the luminosity ceiling L/L_Edd ∝ (1+z)² M_BH^{-1/5} encloses all but 24 of 1.5×10⁵ quasars, and M_BH/σ⁵ stays constant.
- JADES black-hole hosts lie higher in σ³/R_e than local disc hosts measured alike, by +1.18 dex at fixed σ and +1.98 dex at fixed dynamical mass.
